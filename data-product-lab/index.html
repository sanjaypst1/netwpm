Metrics hierarchy

  1. North-star outcome

    Trusted adviser and client decisions, made faster, with less operational friction.

  2. Customer outcomes

    Onboarding cycle time, journey completion, first-time resolution.

  3. Business outcomes

    Avoidable service demand, benefits realised, capacity released.

  4. Product-adoption measures

    Monthly active consumers, repeat use, decisions supported by governed products.

  5. Data-product health

    Named owners, contract coverage, consumer satisfaction.

  6. Data-quality measures

    Completeness, accuracy, freshness, unresolved exceptions.

  7. Operational measures

    Incident rate, availability, time to insight.

  8. Delivery measures

    Release predictability, defect leakage, lead time.

  9. Risk and control measures

    Control exceptions, access violations, overdue evidence.

Governance, risks and responsible AI

  • Building dashboards before agreeing the decision and the consumer.
  • Unclear ownership of critical fields.
  • Privacy and access models that block legitimate use or over-expose PII.
  • Snowflake / Azure depth assumed rather than confirmed in the squad.
  • Success metrics without baselines.
  • AI features introduced before data quality and model governance exist.

Responsible AI

  • Use AI only where a decision, a consumer and a control model exist.
  • Keep humans accountable for advice, client outcomes and regulatory obligations.
  • Do not train or display confidential employer or client data in this portfolio.
  • Model governance, lineage and challenge processes are prerequisites, not extras.
  • This section is industry knowledge and a portfolio demonstration, not employment history.

Future roadmap

  1. Establish baselines and named owners for critical data products.
  2. Deliver a minimum viable onboarding and service-insights product.
  3. Expand adoption analytics once quality and access are trusted.
  4. Only then consider assisted insight or responsible-AI features.
